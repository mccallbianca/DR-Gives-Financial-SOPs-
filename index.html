§ Statement of
Internal Controls

Desert Rose Gives is committed to maintaining the highest standards of financial integrity, transparency, and accountability. As a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization, we recognize that public trust is the foundation of our ability to fulfill our mission — and that trust is earned through rigorous, consistent, and documented financial oversight.

Our internal controls framework is designed to prevent fraud, waste, and abuse; ensure the accuracy and completeness of financial records; protect organizational assets; and ensure compliance with all applicable federal, state, and grantor requirements. These controls are not procedural formalities — they are active safeguards that govern every financial decision made on behalf of this organization.

Segregation of Duties: No single individual has complete control over any significant financial transaction. Approval, execution, and recordkeeping functions are assigned to separate personnel. The Program Manager approves expenditures. The CFO reviews and authorizes payment. Operations (Mario Hernandez) maintains records in QuickBooks. Board oversight applies to expenditures exceeding ,500.

Dual Authorization: All payments above de minimis thresholds require review and approval by both the Program Manager and the CFO before execution. This dual-approval requirement applies to vendor payments, payroll, reimbursements, and grant-funded purchases.

Documentation Standards: Every financial transaction is supported by source documentation — receipts, invoices, contracts, or purchase authorizations — retained in both QuickBooks and the organization's secure digital shared drive. No payment is processed without supporting documentation on file.

Reconciliation and Review: Bank accounts are reconciled monthly. QuickBooks entries are cross-referenced against bank statements and supporting documentation. The CFO reviews all financial activity monthly and reports to the Board on a quarterly basis.

Grant Compliance: Funds received from federal, state, or private grantors are tracked in separate designated accounts or cost centers within QuickBooks. Expenditures are reviewed against approved grant budgets before processing. No grant funds are expended without a fully executed award agreement and Board authorization of the associated program work order.

1.1 — Grant Expenditure Separation and Account Structure
▾

Desert Rose Gives maintains a structured chart of accounts within QuickBooks that separates grant expenditures by funding source, program activity, and expense category. Each active grant is assigned a dedicated cost center or project code, ensuring that all revenues and expenditures associated with a specific award are tracked independently from general organizational operations and from other grant-funded programs.

Within each grant cost center, expenditures are further categorized by type — Personnel, Direct Program Supplies, Technology & Software, Program Operating Expenses, and Contractual Services — mirroring the approved grant budget line items. This structure enables real-time reconciliation of actual spending against the approved budget, facilitates grantor reporting, and ensures that no cross-allocation of funds occurs between separate awards without documented justification and prior approval.

1.2 — Expense Authorization: Permission to Incur Grant Expenditures
▾

No expenditure may be charged to a grant award without a fully executed award agreement on file and Board authorization of the associated program work order. Prior to incurring any grant-funded expense, the following authorization sequence must be completed:

  • The Board reviews and approves the program work order and any associated purchase orders at or before the grant award date.
  • Once the award is executed, the Program Manager holds authority to initiate approved expenditures within pre-authorized categories and amounts.
  • For software, subscriptions, or equipment, the Program Manager completes the purchase following written pre-approval documentation.
  • All receipts and purchase confirmations are submitted to Operations (Mario Hernandez) within 5 business days of the transaction for entry into QuickBooks.
  • The CFO conducts a monthly audit and reconciliation of all grant expenditures against approved budget categories.
Best Practice Note: This sequential authorization process implements the nonprofit internal control principle of pre-approval for grant expenditures, consistent with OMB Uniform Guidance 2 CFR §200.302 (financial management standards) and AICPA nonprofit audit guidance.

1.3 — Travel Authorization and Reimbursement Policy
▾

Travel expenses are not anticipated as part of the current grant program scope. Accordingly, this policy section is designated Not Applicable (N/A) for the current award period.

In the event that travel becomes a program necessity, the following standing policy applies: All travel must be pre-approved in writing by the Program Manager and, for travel costs exceeding 00, by the CFO. Travel arrangements should prioritize the most economical reasonable option. Reimbursement requests must include original receipts, a written business purpose statement, and the Program Manager's signed approval. Reimbursements are processed through Paychex payroll or direct payment in alignment with IRS per diem rates where applicable. No alcohol, personal expenses, or costs outside the approved program scope are reimbursable.

Current program status: Travel not budgeted or anticipated. If travel becomes necessary, a budget modification request will be submitted to the grantor for approval prior to any travel occurring.

1.4 — Purchasing Process: Items, Subscriptions, and Equipment
▾

All purchases made on behalf of Desert Rose Gives require prior written authorization. The purchasing process is as follows:

  • Step 1 — Purchase Request: The employee or contractor submits a written purchase request to the Program Manager identifying the item, vendor, cost, and grant budget line item to which the expense will be charged.
  • Step 2 — Program Manager Review: The Program Manager reviews the request for alignment with approved program activities and budget. If approved, written authorization is issued.
  • Step 3 — Purchase Execution: The purchase is completed either by the employee with prior written approval in hand, or directly by the Program Manager.
  • Step 4 — Documentation: All receipts are submitted to Operations within 5 business days and entered into QuickBooks under the correct grant cost center and category.

1.5 — Expense Coding and Account Allocation
▾

Desert Rose Gives assigns expenses to specific accounts based on the nature of the expense, its direct relationship to program activities, and alignment with the approved grant budget line items. The following allocation principles apply:

  • Software and Subscriptions: Classified under the Program Technology/Operating account, as they directly support program delivery and digital infrastructure.
  • Personnel Costs: Salaries and contracted labor for the Program Manager, Outreach Coordinator, and other grant-funded staff are allocated to the Personnel account, proportional to time spent on grant-funded activities as documented on approved timesheets.
  • Direct Program Supplies: Materials and resources used directly in program delivery are coded to the Direct Program Supplies line.
  • Indirect / Administrative Costs: Allocated in accordance with the indirect cost rate, if any, approved in the grant award. No indirect costs are charged in excess of the approved rate.

All expense coding is performed using the QuickBooks chart of accounts by Operations staff (Mario Hernandez) at the time of entry. The CFO reviews coding accuracy monthly during reconciliation.

1.6 — Invoice Review and Payment Process
▾

Desert Rose Gives employs a dual-review payment process to ensure accuracy, prevent unauthorized payments, and maintain audit-ready documentation for all expenditures.

  • Receipt and Verification: When a bill or invoice is received, the Program Manager first confirms that the goods or services were received, are consistent with the contract or purchase order, and align with the approved grant budget.
  • Financial Review and Approval: The verified invoice is submitted to the CFO for independent financial review. The CFO confirms the expense is allowable, allocable, and reasonable under applicable grant terms and organizational policies.
  • Payment Execution: Upon CFO approval, payment is initiated — typically via ACH electronic transfer or check — by the CFO or designated financial officer. No payment is executed without both the Program Manager verification and CFO approval on file.
  • Recordkeeping: The paid invoice, payment confirmation (bank transaction record or check image), payment date, and both approvals are recorded in QuickBooks and filed in the organization's secure digital records by Operations.
The dual-review requirement — Program Manager verification followed by independent CFO approval — is a core internal control implementing the principle of segregation of duties for disbursements, consistent with AICPA and COSO framework guidance for nonprofits.
🗂️
1.7 — Payment Record Retention
▾

All payment records are maintained in two parallel systems to ensure redundancy, audit readiness, and long-term accessibility:

QuickBooks (Primary)
Paid invoice, proof of payment (bank transaction or check image), payment date, account coding, and reconciliation status. All entries reviewed monthly by CFO.
Digital Records Archive
Secure shared drive managed by Operations (Mario Hernandez). Digital copies of all invoices, purchase authorizations, receipts, and payment confirmations organized by grant award and fiscal year.

Payment records are retained for a minimum of seven (7) years from the date of the final grant report or audit resolution, whichever is later, in compliance with OMB Uniform Guidance 2 CFR §200.334 and IRS nonprofit record retention standards. Records related to real property or equipment are retained for the useful life of the asset plus seven years.

1.8 — Reimbursement Request Process
▾

Desert Rose Gives submits reimbursement requests to grantors on a cost-reimbursement basis. The process is as follows:

  • Documentation Compilation: The Program Manager compiles all approved expenditures incurred during the reporting period, along with corresponding documentation: receipts, invoices, proof of payment, and approved purchase authorizations.
  • CFO Review: The CFO independently reviews the compiled documentation for accuracy, completeness, and compliance with grant terms and approved budget categories. Any discrepancies are resolved before submission.
  • Reimbursement Form Preparation: The CFO completes the grantor's standardized reimbursement form (or the organization's template where no grantor form is provided), attaching all supporting documentation.
  • Submission: The complete reimbursement package is submitted to the funder by the required deadline via the grantor's designated submission method (eCivis, email, portal, or mail).
  • Record Retention: A complete copy of the reimbursement request, all attachments, and the submission confirmation is retained in QuickBooks and the digital records archive.
Reimbursement requests are prepared on the schedule specified in the grant award agreement. Desert Rose Gives does not submit reimbursement requests for expenses that have not been fully paid, received, and documented.
💰
1.9 — Revenue and Reimbursement Receipt Process
▾

All revenues and reimbursements received by Desert Rose Gives are processed through the following protocol to ensure accuracy, proper allocation, and audit traceability:

  • Deposit: All funds — grant reimbursements, donations, program income — are deposited into Desert Rose Gives' designated organizational bank account within 2 business days of receipt.
  • QuickBooks Entry: The Operations team records the deposit in QuickBooks, clearly labeling the source (grant name and award number, donor record, or program income type) and the applicable cost center or fund.
  • Supporting Documentation: Remittance advice, grant payment notifications, donation acknowledgments, or bank deposit confirmations are filed with financial records in the digital archive.
  • CFO Monthly Review: The CFO reviews all revenue deposits monthly to confirm accuracy of amount, source attribution, and proper allocation. All revenues are reconciled against expected receivables and reimbursement submissions.
  • Quarterly Reporting: Revenue activity is included in the CFO's quarterly financial report to the Board.

1.10 — Incorrect Expense Coding: Correction Process
▾

Desert Rose Gives treats coding corrections as compliance-critical events requiring documentation equivalent to the original transaction. The correction process is as follows:

  • Identification: The Program Manager or CFO identifies the coding error — whether discovered through routine review, reconciliation, or audit preparation.
  • CFO Review and Authorization: The CFO independently reviews the error, determines the correct account code based on the chart of accounts and applicable funding source rules, and authorizes the correction.
  • QuickBooks Adjustment: Operations updates the entry with the correct account code and adds a documented note to the transaction record explaining: (a) the original incorrect code, (b) the correct code, (c) the reason for the change, and (d) the name of the person authorizing the correction and the date.
  • Report Updates: Any reports generated prior to the correction — including grant financial reports — are updated to reflect the corrected coding. If a reimbursement request was already submitted with the incorrect coding, a corrected version is submitted to the grantor with a written explanation.
  • Pattern Monitoring: If the same coding error recurs, the CFO reviews the underlying chart of accounts structure and provides retraining to Operations staff to prevent future occurrences.

2.1 — Hiring Process
▾

Desert Rose Gives operates a lean, mission-driven staffing model. The organization engages individuals on a project basis when program funding is secured, drawing primarily from an established statewide network of behavioral health, technology, and community development professionals cultivated through the Founder's work with Nevada agencies and programs.

  • Needs Identification: When a funded project is awarded, the Founder/Program Manager identifies the roles, skills, and qualifications required to execute the program scope of work.
  • Candidate Selection: Candidates are sourced from the organization's professional network, with priority given to individuals whose skills, mission alignment, and relevant experience have been previously vetted.
  • Contract Execution: All engagements — whether employee or independent contractor — are formalized through written agreements clearly defining scope of work, deliverables, timeline, and compensation. No work begins without a signed contract.
  • Compliance Review: The CFO confirms that the position and compensation are within the approved grant budget before the contract is executed. All contracts are retained in organizational records.

All hiring decisions prioritize mission alignment, documented expertise, and the ability to deliver quality, measurable outcomes consistent with funder expectations.

⏱️
2.2 — Timesheets and Payroll Processing
▾

Desert Rose Gives uses QuickBooks and Paychex to manage timekeeping and payroll processing for all project-based staff.

  • Time Tracking: Project-based staff complete timesheets weekly, documenting hours worked specifically by grant activity and program function.
  • Program Manager Review: All timesheets are reviewed and approved by the Program Manager before payroll processing, confirming that documented hours align with the approved scope of work and activity log.
  • Payroll Processing: Approved timesheets are entered into Paychex for payroll processing. Payments are issued via direct deposit on the established payroll schedule.
  • QuickBooks Sync: All payroll data is synced with QuickBooks for financial tracking, grant expense reporting, and audit documentation. Personnel costs are coded to the correct grant cost center based on the approved budget.
Timesheets are considered source documentation for personnel costs charged to grants and are subject to grantor audit. Records are retained for the same period as other grant financial records (minimum 7 years).
📐
2.3 — Position Classification and Compensation Standards
▾

Desert Rose Gives determines position scope and compensation through a structured review process that ensures reasonableness, funding compliance, and mission alignment:

  • Role Definition: The Founder, in consultation with the CFO, defines the duties, required qualifications, and expected deliverables for each position based on the program's scope of work.
  • Compensation Benchmarking: Compensation rates are established using benchmarks from comparable nonprofit roles in Nevada — including data from Candid/GuideStar salary surveys, Nevada nonprofit sector reports, and prevailing rates for similar grant-funded positions. Rates must be reasonable relative to the responsibilities assigned.
  • Budget Alignment: All positions and rates are confirmed to align with the approved grant budget before contracts are finalized. No compensation above the budgeted rate is authorized without a grantor-approved budget modification.
  • CFO Approval: The CFO reviews all compensation determinations for reasonableness and compliance prior to contract execution.

Compensation for independent contractors is set per project deliverable or hourly rate as specified in the contract. All compensation is documented and retained as part of the personnel file for each engagement.

🎯
2.4 — Grant Time Allocation Accuracy
▾

The accuracy of personnel time charged to grants is a core compliance requirement. Desert Rose Gives implements the following controls to ensure that only eligible, accurate time is expensed to any grant award:

  • Activity-Based Timekeeping: Staff track time by specific grant activity — not only by grant — documenting what work was performed alongside hours logged. Timesheets reflect actual hours worked, not estimated or scheduled hours.
  • Program Manager Review: The Program Manager reviews each timesheet for alignment with the approved scope of work and documented program activities before approving for payroll.
  • CFO Verification: The CFO independently verifies that time charged to the grant matches the approved budget, the timesheet documentation, and the allowable activities defined in the grant agreement.
  • Cross-Reference: Payroll records and QuickBooks entries are cross-referenced quarterly to confirm that only eligible time has been expensed to the grant and that no overcharging has occurred.
  • Effort Certification: For federally funded grants, semi-annual effort certifications or equivalent documentation are prepared per applicable OMB guidance (2 CFR §200.430) and retained with personnel records.

3.1 — Inventory Review and Property Controls
▾

Desert Rose Gives does not maintain a substantial standing inventory. The organization's program model is primarily digital and service-based, with program-specific materials distributed directly to participants on a pass-through basis. Accordingly, inventory is tracked per program and reconciled at the close of each funded project cycle.

  • Inventory Review Timing: A full inventory review is completed at program closeout or when final reports are prepared for each funded cycle. Interim reviews are conducted upon request or when a discrepancy is identified.
  • Last Review: The most recent inventory review was completed following the close of the organization's most recent funded program cycle.
  • Equipment Tracking: Any equipment purchased with grant funds is assigned an asset tag, entered into the property log at the time of purchase, and subject to the disposal and transfer policies required by the funding source. Equipment with a per-unit cost of ,000 or more (federal threshold) or as specified in the grant award is tracked on a depreciation schedule.
  • Disposition: Disposition of grant-funded equipment at program close follows the requirements of 2 CFR §200.313 for federal awards, or the specific requirements of the funding source for non-federal awards. No grant-funded equipment is sold, transferred, or disposed of without prior written authorization from the grantor.
Given the current program's digital infrastructure focus, equipment purchases are not anticipated. Should equipment be acquired under a grant award, the full property management protocol above applies from the date of purchase.
